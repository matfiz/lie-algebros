Nilpotency and Engel’s theorem

Iterated brackets define nilpotency of an abstract Lie algebra. Engel’s theorem detects this property through nilpotent operators. We prove the linear form first. We then apply it to ad⁡\adad and follow the proof in the strictly upper triangular 4×44\times44×4 matrices.

Vector spaces and Lie algebras are finite-dimensional over C\CC. A linear map z ⁣:V→Vz\colon V\to Vz:V→V is nilpotent if zm=0z^m=0zm=0 for some m≥1m\ge1m≥1.

2.4 Nilpotent algebras and nilpotent matrices

Let ρ ⁣:g→gl(V)\rho\colon\g\to\gl(V)ρ:g→gl(V) be a representation with V≠0V\ne0V=0. Two conditions are easily confused.

  1. The Lie algebra g\gg is nilpotent.

  2. Every operator ρ(x)\rho(x)ρ(x) is nilpotent.

Condition (B) says that ρ(g)\rho(\g)ρ(g) satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem 2.8. Then VVV has a common null vector and a full flag lowered by every ρ(x)\rho(x)ρ(x). Moreover ρ(g)≅g/ker⁡ρ\rho(\g)\cong\g/\ker\rhoρ(g)≅g/kerρ is nilpotent by Corollary 2.10. If ρ\rhoρ is faithful, (B) implies (A). In general, neither condition implies the other.

Summary

The lower central series records iterated brackets. Subalgebras and quotients of nilpotent algebras are nilpotent. A nilpotent central quotient forces nilpotency.

Engel’s linear theorem gives a Lie algebra of nilpotent operators on a nonzero space a common null vector and a lowered full flag. Applied to ad⁡g≅g/Z(g)\ad\g\cong\g/Z(\g)adg≅g/Z(g), it shows that a Lie algebra is nilpotent exactly when all its elements are ad-nilpotent. A nilpotent algebra may still act by non-nilpotent matrices. Lecture 3 replaces null vectors by common eigenvectors.
