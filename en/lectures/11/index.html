Lecture 11

Universal enveloping algebras and PBW

What this lecture does

A representation turns brackets into commutators of operators. We build the associative algebra that does this universally. Then we prove that its ordered monomials form a basis.

11.1 The enveloping algebra

For a vector space VVV, the tensor algebra is T(V)=⨁k≥0V⊗k,V⊗0=C,T(V)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V^{\otimes k},\qquad V^{\otimes0}=\C,T(V)=k≥0⨁​V⊗k,V⊗0=C, with product given on pure tensors by concatenation. Multilinear algebra gives its universal property. A linear map φ ⁣:V→A\varphi\colon V\to Aφ:V→A into an algebra extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism T(V)→AT(V)\to AT(V)→A, given by v1⊗⋯⊗vk⟼φ(v1)⋯φ(vk).v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_k\longmapsto\varphi(v_1)\cdots\varphi(v_k).v1​⊗⋯⊗vk​⟼φ(v1​)⋯φ(vk​).

Every algebra AAA is a Lie algebra under [a,b]=ab−ba[a,b]=ab-ba[a,b]=ab−ba. The Jacobi identity holds by the expansion in Lecture 1.

Definition 11.1 (Universal enveloping algebra). Let III be the two-sided ideal of T(g)T(\g)T(g) generated by the elements x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y](x,y∈g).x\otimes y-y\otimes x-[x,y]\qquad(x,y\in\g).x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y](x,y∈g). Let ι ⁣:g→U(g)\iota\colon\g\to U(\g)ι:g→U(g) be the inclusion g=g⊗1⊆T(g)\g=\g^{\otimes1}\subseteq T(\g)g=g⊗1⊆T(g) followed by the quotient map.

By construction, ι(x)ι(y)−ι(y)ι(x)=ι([x,y])for all x,y∈g.\iota(x)\iota(y)-\iota(y)\iota(x)=\iota([x,y]) \quad\text{for all }x,y\in\g.ι(x)ι(y)−ι(y)ι(x)=ι([x,y])for all x,y∈g. Proposition 11.2 (Universal property). Let AAA be an algebra and φ ⁣:g→A\varphi\colon\g\to Aφ:g→A a linear map with φ([x,y])=φ(x)φ(y)−φ(y)φ(x)for all x,y∈g.\varphi([x,y])=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)-\varphi(y)\varphi(x) \quad\text{for all }x,y\in\g.φ([x,y])=φ(x)φ(y)−φ(y)φ(x)for all x,y∈g. Proof. Let Φ ⁣:T(g)→A\Phi\colon T(\g)\to AΦ:T(g)→A extend φ\varphiφ. On a generator of III, Φ(x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y])=φ(x)φ(y)−φ(y)φ(x)−φ([x,y])=0.\Phi(x\otimes y-y\otimes x-[x,y]) =\varphi(x)\varphi(y)-\varphi(y)\varphi(x)-\varphi([x,y])=0.Φ(x⊗y−y⊗x−[x,y])=φ(x)φ(y)−φ(y)φ(x)−φ([x,y])=0. The algebra U(g)U(\g)U(g) is generated by ι(g)\iota(\g)ι(g), because T(g)T(\g)T(g) is generated by g\gg. Two homomorphisms that agree on ι(g)\iota(\g)ι(g) are therefore equal.

Spanning is proved. For independence we build a module on which the ordered monomials act independently.

One example shows why independence needs an argument. Take sl2\sllie_2sl2​ with the ordered basis (f,h,e)(f,h,e)(f,h,e) and the rewriting rules eh=he−2e,hf=fh−2f,ef=fe+h,eh=he-2e,\qquad hf=fh-2f,\qquad ef=fe+h,eh=he−2e,hf=fh−2f,ef=fe+h, which §11.5 derives from the brackets. The word ehfehfehf has two out-of-order neighbouring pairs, so reordering can begin in two ways. Figure 11.1 shows both routes. They end at the same ordered expression. If they ended at different ordered expressions, the difference would be a nontrivial linear relation among ordered monomials. This example is a preview and not part of the proof. The general compatibility is proved in §11.3, where it uses the Jacobi identity.

A natural place to stop. We have built U(g)U(\g)U(g), shown that its ordered monomials span it, and stated the PBW theorem. The next part proves that these monomials are independent, by constructing a module on polynomials.

Summary

The enveloping algebra U(g)U(\g)U(g) turns brackets into commutators. Its unital modules are exactly the g\gg-modules.

Reordering proves that the ordered monomials span U(g)U(\g)U(g). Their independence comes from a polynomial module whose construction uses the Jacobi identity. Two consequences are gr⁡U(g)≅Sym⁡(g)\operatorname{gr}U(\g)\cong\operatorname{Sym}(\g)grU(g)≅Sym(g) and, for semisimple g\gg, U(g)≅U(n−)⊗U(b)U(\g)\cong U(\mathfrak{n}_-)\otimes U(\borel)U(g)≅U(n−​)⊗U(b) as vector spaces. The second is the input for Verma modules in Lecture 12.
