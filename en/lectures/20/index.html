Type A branching and Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns

What this lecture does

We restrict the polynomial gln\gl_ngln​-modules of Lecture 18 to the upper-left block gln−1\gl_{n-1}gln−1​. Each summand occurs at most once. Repeating this along a chain of blocks splits a module into canonical lines. Restriction to sln−1\sllie_{n-1}sln−1​ alone can repeat a summand.

Summary

Restricting Sλ(Cn)\mathbb S_\lambda(\C^n)Sλ​(Cn) to gln−1\gl_{n-1}gln−1​ gives each Sμ(Cn−1)\mathbb S_\mu(\C^{n-1})Sμ​(Cn−1) with μ≺λ\mu\prec\lambdaμ≺λ exactly once. The proof combines the interlacing identity, complete reducibility and the central scalars ∣μ∣|\mu|∣μ∣.

Iterating along gl1⊂⋯⊂gln\gl_1\subset\cdots\subset\gl_ngl1​⊂⋯⊂gln​ gives canonical lines indexed by Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns. Basis vectors in them are fixed only up to independent scalars. Over sln−1\sllie_{n-1}sln−1​, summands whose labels differ by full columns become isomorphic, as the two copies of V1V_1V1​ in sl3\sllie_3sl3​ show.
