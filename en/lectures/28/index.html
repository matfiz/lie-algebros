The centre of U(g)U(\g)U(g) and central characters

What this lecture does

Central elements of U(g)U(\g)U(g) act by scalars on highest-weight modules. We compute these scalars, find the whole centre for sl2\sllie_2sl2​ and state Harish-Chandra’s theorem. A central character depends on a highest weight λ\lambdaλ only through the Weyl orbit of λ+ρ\lambda+\rhoλ+ρ. We compare this orbit with the Casimir sphere of Lecture 16. The lecture ends by collecting what a highest-weight label gives.

Summary

On a highest-weight module, a central element zzz acts by χλ(z)=γ(z)(λ+ρ)\chi_\lambda(z)=\gamma(z)(\lambda+\rho)χλ​(z)=γ(z)(λ+ρ). Harish-Chandra’s isomorphism makes these scalars depend exactly on the orbit W(λ+ρ)W(\lambda+\rho)W(λ+ρ). They separate finite-dimensional simple modules. The nonsplit sequence 0→M(−m−2)→M(m)→L(m)→00\to M(-m-2)\to M(m)\to L(m)\to00→M(−m−2)→M(m)→L(m)→0 above shows their limits in infinite dimension. For λ∈P\lambda\in Pλ∈P, the value on Ω\OmegaΩ restricts λ+ρ\lambda+\rhoλ+ρ to a sphere in the real weight space. This sphere can contain several Weyl orbits.
