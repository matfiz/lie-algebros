28.6 Synthesis and the two sequels

Lie groups. The first sequel fixes a complex Lie group GGG with Lie algebra g\gg. It asks which L(λ)L(\lambda)L(λ) arise from representations of GGG. The answer depends on GGG.

Quantum groups. The sequel deforms the relations of U(g)U(\g)U(g) by a parameter qqq. It asks how tensor products and complete reducibility change. It develops a parameter-dependent rule for exchanging tensor factors in place of the flip of Lecture 14, with precise conditions on qqq. At roots of unity, complete reducibility can fail. These are questions for the sequels; no result here depends on them.

Summary

On a highest-weight module, a central element zzz acts by χλ(z)=γ(z)(λ+ρ)\chi_\lambda(z)=\gamma(z)(\lambda+\rho)χλ​(z)=γ(z)(λ+ρ). Harish-Chandra’s isomorphism makes these scalars depend exactly on the orbit W(λ+ρ)W(\lambda+\rho)W(λ+ρ). They separate finite-dimensional simple modules. The nonsplit Verma extension above shows their limits in infinite dimension.
