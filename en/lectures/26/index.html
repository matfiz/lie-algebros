Minuscule shortcuts

This subsection is a sketch [S]. Call L(λ)L(\lambda)L(λ) minuscule if its weights form one Weyl orbit. Multiplicities are Weyl-invariant and dim⁡L(λ)λ=1\dim L(\lambda)_\lambda=1dimL(λ)λ​=1, so every multiplicity is 111 and the character is the orbit sum. The spin and half-spin modules are minuscule by their weight lists in Lecture 24 and the Weyl actions of Lecture 9. Summary

Orthogonal restriction along Embeddings I and II, the stabilisers of nonisotropic vectors, is multiplicity-free. The highest weights of the summands interlace with the given one and have its type.

The spin cases follow from the exterior model. Parity splits the BnB_nBn​ spinor, and γ(u)\gamma(u)γ(u) identifies the two DnD_nDn​ half-spinors over Bn−1B_{n-1}Bn−1​.

Symplectic restriction to the block sp2n−2\mathfrak{sp}_{2n-2}sp2n−2​ has a product formula with multiplicities above one. Every comparison begins by naming the embedding.
