Lecture 15

Formal characters and the Weyl denominator

The formal character of a module collects the dimensions of its weight spaces. We prove the rules for characters and show that a character determines a finite-dimensional module. We then compute the character of a Verma module and prove the Weyl denominator identity. These are the inputs of the character formula in Lecture 16.

Subtracting characters

Characters are elements of the abelian group E\mathcal{E}E. An identity there is an identity of integer-valued functions on PPP. Subtraction is defined in E\mathcal{E}E, but a difference of characters need not be the character of a module.

For an exact sequence 0→U→V→X→00\to U\to V\to X\to00→U→V→X→0 of finite-dimensional modules, additivity gives ch⁡V−ch⁡U=ch⁡X\operatorname{ch}V-\operatorname{ch}U=\operatorname{ch}XchV−chU=chX. More generally, let fff be a difference of characters of finite-dimensional modules. By complete reducibility, fff is an integer combination of the characters ch⁡L(λ)\operatorname{ch}L(\lambda)chL(λ), λ∈P+\lambda\in P_+λ∈P+​. The coefficients are unique, by the linear independence shown in the proof of Proposition 15.5. Hence fff is the character of a finite-dimensional module exactly when all these coefficients are nonnegative. Nonnegative WWW-invariant weight coefficients do not suffice. Exercise 15.1 gives an example.

Summary

For a finite-dimensional module, the formal character records the dimensions of its weight spaces. It is additive, multiplicative and Weyl-invariant. It determines the module up to isomorphism. A difference of finite-dimensional characters is the character of a module exactly when its coefficients in the irreducible characters are nonnegative. The PBW basis gives ch⁡M(λ)=∑γp(γ)eλ−γ\operatorname{ch}M(\lambda)=\sum_\gamma p(\gamma)e^{\lambda-\gamma}chM(λ)=∑γ​p(γ)eλ−γ for λ∈P\lambda\in Pλ∈P in the completion E\mathcal{E}E, whose elements are supported in finitely many cones.

The Weyl denominator identity follows from anti-invariance, the support bound and positivity of the form. Together with the Verma character, it shows that AρA_\rhoAρ​ is the inverse of ch⁡M(−ρ)\operatorname{ch}M(-\rho)chM(−ρ).
