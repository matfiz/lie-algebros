At most three edges, counted with multiplicity, meet at a node, so a triple edge occurs only in G2G_2G2​. There is at most one multiple edge or branch node in total, and the arm lengths are restricted. Omitted step: these proofs and the final case analysis (Humphreys §11.4; Erdmann–Wildon, Chapter 13).

A root system is irreducible if it is not the union of two nonempty orthogonal subsets. Equivalently, its diagram is connected [C: Humphreys §11.3].

Source: Humphreys §§14.2 and 18.3–18.4. His printed matrix is transposed.

Root-system classification uses Euclidean geometry. Serre’s theorem supplies existence and uniqueness of the algebra with the prescribed Cartan matrix. Its proof works with generators and relations for a given root system. It is not a case analysis of diagrams. We cite it because that construction is long. Lecture 25 uses the theorem to turn a symmetry of the diagram D4D_4D4​ into automorphisms of the algebra.

By a cited theorem [C: Humphreys §14.1], a semisimple algebra is simple exactly when its root system is irreducible. Hence each complex simple Lie algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of exactly one type in the table. For types AAA–DDD it is a matrix algebra of §10.2.

Summary

A base gives a Cartan matrix, and the diagram records the same data. Positivity of qΓq_\GammaqΓ​ is the test behind the classification, which restricts connected diagrams to types AAA–GGG. The classical algebras realise AAA–DDD. Serre’s theorem shows that the Cartan matrix determines the algebra. With the classification theorem, it shows that every type occurs.
