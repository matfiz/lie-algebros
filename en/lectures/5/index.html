Lecture 5

Casimir operators and complete reducibility

What this lecture does

Lecture 3 gave a module with an invariant line and no invariant complement. We prove that this cannot happen for a semisimple Lie algebra. The tool is the Casimir operator, built from the trace form of the module.

Throughout, Lie algebras and modules are finite-dimensional over C\CC. Recall from Lecture 4 that g\gg is semisimple if it has no nonzero solvable ideal.

5.1 Trace forms of representations

Definition 5.1 (Trace form). Let ρ ⁣:g→gl(V)\rho\colon\g\to\gl(V)ρ:g→gl(V) be a representation. The trace form of VVV is βV(x,y)=tr⁡(ρ(x)ρ(y)),x,y∈g.\beta_V(x,y)=\tr\bigl(\rho(x)\rho(y)\bigr),\qquad x,y\in\g.βV​(x,y)=tr(ρ(x)ρ(y)),x,y∈g.

The trace form is bilinear and symmetric, since tr⁡(AB)=tr⁡(BA)\tr(AB)=\tr(BA)tr(AB)=tr(BA). For the adjoint module it is the Killing form κ\kappaκ of Lecture 4.

Lemma 5.2 (Invariance[P]). For all x,y,z∈gx,y,z\in\gx,y,z∈g, βV([x,y],z)=βV(x,[y,z]).\beta_V([x,y],z)=\beta_V(x,[y,z]).βV​([x,y],z)=βV​(x,[y,z]).

Proof. For A,B,C∈End⁡(V)A,B,C\in\End(V)A,B,C∈End(V), cyclicity of the trace gives tr⁡(BAC)=tr⁡(ACB)\tr(BAC)=\tr(ACB)tr(BAC)=tr(ACB). Hence tr⁡([A,B]C)=tr⁡(ABC)−tr⁡(BAC)=tr⁡(ABC)−tr⁡(ACB)=tr⁡(A[B,C]).\tr([A,B]C)=\tr(ABC)-\tr(BAC)=\tr(ABC)-\tr(ACB)=\tr(A[B,C]).tr([A,B]C)=tr(ABC)−tr(BAC)=tr(ABC)−tr(ACB)=tr(A[B,C]). Put A=ρ(x)A=\rho(x)A=ρ(x), B=ρ(y)B=\rho(y)B=ρ(y), C=ρ(z)C=\rho(z)C=ρ(z), and use ρ([x,y])=[ρ(x),ρ(y)]\rho([x,y])=[\rho(x),\rho(y)]ρ([x,y])=[ρ(x),ρ(y)]. 

Invariance and the antisymmetry of the bracket give the identity used below: βV([z,x],y)=−βV([x,z],y)=−βV(x,[z,y]).\begin{equation} \beta_V([z,x],y)=-\beta_V([x,z],y)=-\beta_V(x,[z,y]). \label{lec5:eq:skew} \end{equation}βV​([z,x],y)=−βV​([x,z],y)=−βV​(x,[z,y]).(5.1)

A trace form can be degenerate. Its radical always contains ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ. For semisimple g\gg, Cartan’s criterion shows that the radical is exactly ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ.

Theorem 5.3 (Cartan’s criterion[C]). Let VVV be a finite-dimensional complex vector space and let L⊆gl(V)L\subseteq\gl(V)L⊆gl(V) be a Lie subalgebra. If tr⁡(AB)=0for all A∈[L,L], B∈L,\tr(AB)=0\quad\text{for all }A\in[L,L],\ B\in L,tr(AB)=0for all A∈[L,L], B∈L, then LLL is solvable.

This is the Theorem in Humphreys, Section 4.3. Lecture 4 stated it. We also use two results proved in Lecture 4. Let g\gg be semisimple.

  1. If a\algaa is an ideal and a⊥\alga^\perpa⊥ is its orthogonal space for κ\kappaκ, then a⊥\alga^\perpa⊥ is an ideal and g=a⊕a⊥\g=\alga\oplus\alga^\perpg=a⊕a⊥.

  2. The algebra g\gg is a direct sum of simple ideals g1⊕⋯⊕gr\g_1\oplus\dots\oplus\g_rg1​⊕⋯⊕gr​.

Lemma 5.4 (Perfectness[P]). Let g\gg be semisimple. Then [g,g]=g[\g,\g]=\g[g,g]=g. For every module VVV, ρ(g)⊆sl(V)\rho(\g)\subseteq\sllie(V)ρ(g)⊆sl(V). In particular, g\gg acts by zero on every one-dimensional module.

Proof. Each gi\g_igi​ in (b) is simple, hence nonabelian. Its derived algebra is a nonzero ideal of gi\g_igi​, so [gi,gi]=gi[\g_i,\g_i]=\g_i[gi​,gi​]=gi​. Thus [g,g][\g,\g][g,g] contains every gi\g_igi​, and [g,g]=g[\g,\g]=\g[g,g]=g.

Consequently ρ(g)\rho(\g)ρ(g) is spanned by the operators ρ([x,y])=[ρ(x),ρ(y)]\rho([x,y])=[\rho(x),\rho(y)]ρ([x,y])=[ρ(x),ρ(y)], which are traceless. On a one-dimensional space a traceless operator is zero. 

Proposition 5.5 (The faithful quotient[P]). Let g\gg be semisimple, let ρ ⁣:g→gl(V)\rho\colon\g\to\gl(V)ρ:g→gl(V) be a representation, and let k=(ker⁡ρ)⊥\algk=(\ker\rho)^\perpk=(kerρ)⊥ be the orthogonal space of ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ for κ\kappaκ. Then:

  1. g=ker⁡ρ⊕k\g=\ker\rho\oplus\algkg=kerρ⊕k and [ker⁡ρ,k]=0[\ker\rho,\algk]=0[kerρ,k]=0;

  2. ρ\rhoρ restricts to a Lie algebra isomorphism k→ρ(g)\algk\to\rho(\g)k→ρ(g), so k≅g/ker⁡ρ\algk\cong\g/\ker\rhok≅g/kerρ;

  3. βV\beta_VβV​ restricted to k×k\algk\times\algkk×k is nondegenerate.

Proof. Part (i) is result (a) for the ideal ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ. The bracket [ker⁡ρ,k][\ker\rho,\algk][kerρ,k] lies in both ideals, and their intersection is zero.

For (ii), k∩ker⁡ρ=0\algk\cap\ker\rho=0k∩kerρ=0 gives injectivity. Surjectivity follows from ρ(g)=ρ(ker⁡ρ)+ρ(k)=ρ(k).\rho(\g)=\rho(\ker\rho)+\rho(\algk)=\rho(\algk).ρ(g)=ρ(kerρ)+ρ(k)=ρ(k).

For (iii), let S={x∈k:βV(x,y)=0 for all y∈k}.S=\{x\in\algk:\beta_V(x,y)=0\text{ for all }y\in\algk\}.S={x∈k:βV​(x,y)=0 for all y∈k}. Let x∈Sx\in Sx∈S and y,z∈ky,z\in\algky,z∈k. By (i), [ker⁡ρ,S]=0[\ker\rho,S]=0[kerρ,S]=0. Hence SSS is an ideal of g\gg.

Apply Cartan’s criterion to L=ρ(S)L=\rho(S)L=ρ(S). For x,y∈Sx,y\in Sx,y∈S, tr⁡(ρ(x)ρ(y))=βV(x,y)=0.\tr\bigl(\rho(x)\rho(y)\bigr)=\beta_V(x,y)=0.tr(ρ(x)ρ(y))=βV​(x,y)=0. Since [L,L]⊆L[L,L]\subseteq L[L,L]⊆L, the hypothesis holds, and ρ(S)\rho(S)ρ(S) is solvable. By (ii), S≅ρ(S)S\cong\rho(S)S≅ρ(S). Thus SSS is a solvable ideal of g\gg, so S=0S=0S=0. 

The ideal k\algkk is the faithful quotient g/ker⁡ρ\g/\ker\rhog/kerρ, realised inside g\gg. Since ρ\rhoρ vanishes on ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ and βV\beta_VβV​ is nondegenerate on k\algkk, the radical of βV\beta_VβV​ on all of g=ker⁡ρ⊕k\g=\ker\rho\oplus\algkg=kerρ⊕k is exactly ker⁡ρ\ker\rhokerρ.

5.2 The Casimir operator

Keep the notation of Proposition 5.5 and let m=dim⁡km=\dim\algkm=dimk. Choose a basis (x1,…,xm)(x_1,\dots,x_m)(x1​,…,xm​) of k\algkk. Since βV\beta_VβV​ is nondegenerate on k\algkk, there is a unique dual basis (y1,…,ym)(y_1,\dots,y_m)(y1​,…,ym​) of k\algkk with βV(xi,yj)=δij.\beta_V(x_i,y_j)=\delta_{ij}.βV​(xi​,yj​)=δij​.

Definition 5.6 (Casimir operator). The Casimir operator of the module VVV is cV=∑i=1mρ(xi)ρ(yi)∈End⁡(V).c_V=\sum_{i=1}^m\rho(x_i)\rho(y_i)\in\End(V).cV​=i=1∑m​ρ(xi​)ρ(yi​)∈End(V).

If ρ=0\rho=0ρ=0, then k=0\algk=0k=0 and cV=0c_V=0cV​=0. The operator does not depend on the chosen basis. Pairing with yjy_jyj​, respectively xjx_jxj​, shows that every u∈ku\in\algku∈k satisfies u=∑iβV(u,yi) xi=∑iβV(u,xi) yi.u=\sum_i\beta_V(u,y_i)\,x_i=\sum_i\beta_V(u,x_i)\,y_i.u=i∑​βV​(u,yi​)xi​=i∑​βV​(u,xi​)yi​. Let (xj′)(x'_j)(xj′​), (yj′)(y'_j)(yj′​) be another dual pair. In k⊗k\algk\otimes\algkk⊗k, expand xj′x'_jxj′​ by the first rule, then use the second rule for the pair (xj′),(yj′)(x'_j),(y'_j)(xj′​),(yj′​): ∑jxj′⊗yj′=∑ixi⊗(∑jβV(yi,xj′) yj′)=∑ixi⊗yi.\sum_j x'_j\otimes y'_j=\sum_i x_i\otimes\Bigl(\sum_j\beta_V(y_i,x'_j)\,y'_j\Bigr)=\sum_i x_i\otimes y_i.j∑​xj′​⊗yj′​=i∑​xi​⊗(j∑​βV​(yi​,xj′​)yj′​)=i∑​xi​⊗yi​. The linear map k⊗k→End⁡(V)\algk\otimes\algk\to\End(V)k⊗k→End(V), a⊗b↦ρ(a)ρ(b)a\otimes b\mapsto\rho(a)\rho(b)a⊗b↦ρ(a)ρ(b), turns this into equality of the two operators.

Theorem 5.7 (Casimir operator[P]). Let g\gg be semisimple and let VVV be a module. Then:

  1. cVρ(z)=ρ(z)cVc_V\rho(z)=\rho(z)c_VcV​ρ(z)=ρ(z)cV​ for all z∈gz\in\gz∈g;

  2. tr⁡cV=dim⁡k\tr c_V=\dim\algktrcV​=dimk;

  3. cVc_VcV​ maps every submodule of VVV into itself, and if VVV is irreducible, then cV=dim⁡kdim⁡V id⁡V.c_V=\frac{\dim\algk}{\dim V}\,\id_V.cV​=dimVdimk​idV​.

Proof. If z∈ker⁡ρz\in\ker\rhoz∈kerρ, then ρ(z)=0\rho(z)=0ρ(z)=0. Since g=ker⁡ρ⊕k\g=\ker\rho\oplus\algkg=kerρ⊕k, it remains to take z∈kz\in\algkz∈k. Since k\algkk is an ideal, we can write [z,xi]=∑jaijxj,[z,yi]=∑jbijyj.[z,x_i]=\sum_j a_{ij}x_j,\qquad [z,y_i]=\sum_j b_{ij}y_j.[z,xi​]=j∑​aij​xj​,[z,yi​]=j∑​bij​yj​. Pairing with dual basis vectors and using (5.1) gives aik=βV([z,xi],yk)=−βV(xi,[z,yk])=−bki.a_{ik}=\beta_V([z,x_i],y_k)=-\beta_V(x_i,[z,y_k])=-b_{ki}.aik​=βV​([z,xi​],yk​)=−βV​(xi​,[z,yk​])=−bki​. The identity [A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C][A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C][A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C] in End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) now gives [ρ(z),cV]=∑i(ρ([z,xi])ρ(yi)+ρ(xi)ρ([z,yi]))=∑i,jaij ρ(xj)ρ(yi)+∑i,jbij ρ(xi)ρ(yj)=∑i,j(aji+bij)ρ(xi)ρ(yj)=0.\begin{aligned} [\rho(z),c_V] &=\sum_i\bigl(\rho([z,x_i])\rho(y_i)+\rho(x_i)\rho([z,y_i])\bigr)\\ &=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\rho(x_j)\rho(y_i) +\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\,\rho(x_i)\rho(y_j)\\ &=\sum_{i,j}\bigl(a_{ji}+b_{ij}\bigr)\rho(x_i)\rho(y_j)=0. \end{aligned}[ρ(z),cV​]​=i∑​(ρ([z,xi​])ρ(yi​)+ρ(xi​)ρ([z,yi​]))=i,j∑​aij​ρ(xj​)ρ(yi​)+i,j∑​bij​ρ(xi​)ρ(yj​)=i,j∑​(aji​+bij​)ρ(xi​)ρ(yj​)=0.​ The last line renames i↔ji\leftrightarrow ji↔j in the first sum. The coefficients vanish, since aji=−bija_{ji}=-b_{ij}aji​=−bij​. This proves (i).

For (ii), linearity of the trace gives tr⁡cV=∑i=1mβV(xi,yi)=m=dim⁡k.\tr c_V=\sum_{i=1}^m\beta_V(x_i,y_i)=m=\dim\algk.trcV​=i=1∑m​βV​(xi​,yi​)=m=dimk.

For (iii), a submodule is preserved by each ρ(xi)\rho(x_i)ρ(xi​) and ρ(yi)\rho(y_i)ρ(yi​), hence by cVc_VcV​. If VVV is irreducible, (i) and Schur’s lemma make cVc_VcV​ a scalar. Its value follows from (ii) by taking traces. 

Remark 5.8 (How the form depends on VVV). The commutation proof also works with a basis of g\gg and its dual for any invariant nondegenerate form on g\gg. The form βV\beta_VβV​, the ideal k\algkk and cVc_VcV​ depend on VVV. By Theorem 5.7(ii), the trace of cVc_VcV​ is dim⁡k\dim\algkdimk. This is nonzero when ρ≠0\rho\ne0ρ=0, as needed in Lemma 5.9.

5.3 Splitting a codimension-one submodule

Lemma 5.9 (Irreducible submodule of codimension one[P]). Let g\gg be semisimple, let VVV be a module, and let W⊆VW\subseteq VW⊆V be an irreducible submodule with dim⁡V/W=1\dim V/W=1dimV/W=1. Then V=W⊕XV=W\oplus XV=W⊕X for some submodule XXX.

Proof. If ρ=0\rho=0ρ=0, every subspace is a submodule, and any complementary line works. Assume ρ≠0\rho\ne0ρ=0, so k≠0\algk\ne0k=0.

Put X=ker⁡cVX=\ker c_VX=kercV​. It is a submodule, because cVc_VcV​ commutes with ρ(g)\rho(\g)ρ(g). It meets WWW only in zero, because cVc_VcV​ acts on WWW by λ≠0\lambda\ne0λ=0. 5.4 Weyl’s theorem

Complete reducibility follows by induction on dim⁡V\dim VdimV. The zero module is the empty direct sum, and an irreducible module is its own decomposition. Otherwise choose a submodule WWW with 0≠W≠V0\ne W\ne V0=W=V. Then V=W⊕XV=W\oplus XV=W⊕X, and WWW and XXX are modules of smaller dimension. 

The proof uses linear algebra, Cartan’s criterion, Schur’s lemma and the two results on ideals quoted from Lecture 4. An algebraic proof of this kind goes back to Casimir and van der Waerden.

Semisimplicity entered twice. It gave the nondegenerate trace form of Proposition 5.5, and the trivial action on one-dimensional modules in Lemma 5.4.

Summary

A module VVV carries an invariant trace form. By Cartan’s criterion, for semisimple g\gg this form is nondegenerate on the faithful quotient k≅g/ker⁡ρ\algk\cong\g/\ker\rhok≅g/kerρ. The dual-basis Casimir cVc_VcV​ commutes with the action and has trace dim⁡k\dim\algkdimk.

For a nonzero action, the kernel of the Casimir complements an irreducible submodule of codimension one. For a zero action, any complement is invariant. Induction treats every submodule of codimension one. The module Hom⁡C(V,W)\Hom_\C(V,W)HomC​(V,W) reduces an arbitrary submodule to that case. Hence finite-dimensional modules of semisimple algebras are completely reducible. Lecture 6 uses this to decompose sl2\sllie_2sl2​-modules and their tensor products.
