17.5 Decomposing a module by character subtraction

A difference of characters is an element of Z[P]W\mathbb Z[P]^WZ[P]W (Lecture 15). The next result shows how to subtract so that the result is again the character of a module.

Characters are WWW-invariant, and every orbit in PPP meets P+P_+P+​ (Lecture 9). A nonzero module therefore has a dominant weight. Its finite weight set has a maximal dominant weight. It suffices to record multiplicities of dominant weights.

Summary

A ring homomorphism Φρ\Phi_\rhoΦρ​ to power series turns the character formula into a comparison of leading terms. Because ρ\rhoρ is regular, this gives the Weyl dimension formula, a product of ratios of coroot pairings over the positive roots.

Inverting the denominator in the completed character ring turns the character formula into Kostant’s formula. It is an alternating sum of ∣W∣|W|∣W∣ partition-function values, and the support of ppp discards most terms.

By complete reducibility, subtracting irreducible characters from the top dominant weight down decomposes a module. Lecture 18 compares these formulas with Schur functions in type AAA.
