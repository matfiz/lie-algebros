Summary

The operators ρ(e)\rho(e)ρ(e) and ρ(f)\rho(f)ρ(f) change weights by 222 and −2-2−2. In a finite-dimensional module, the fff-string of a highest vector of weight λ\lambdaλ must stop. If it has m+1m+1m+1 nonzero vectors, the coefficient (m+1)(λ−m)(m+1)(\lambda-m)(m+1)(λ−m) forces λ=m∈{0,1,2,… }\lambda=m\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}λ=m∈{0,1,2,…}, and the string spans a copy of VmV_mVm​. Hence every finite-dimensional irreducible module is isomorphic to exactly one VmV_mVm​.

By Weyl’s theorem, weight dimensions determine every finite-dimensional module up to isomorphism. This gives the Clebsch–Gordan rule. The Casimir operator acts on VmV_mVm​ by m(m+2)/2m(m+2)/2m(m+2)/2. Lecture 8 applies these results to the subalgebras attached to roots.
