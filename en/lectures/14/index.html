14.1 Kernels, cokernels and Hom spaces

Throughout, g\gg is a complex semisimple Lie algebra. Fix a Cartan subalgebra h\mathfrak hh, positive roots R+R_+R+​, simple roots α1,…,αr\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_rα1​,…,αr​ and the Weyl group WWW. Let Vect⁡\operatorname{Vect}Vect be the category of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces and linear maps. Let F ⁣:C→Vect⁡F\colon\mathcal C\to\operatorname{Vect}F:C→Vect be the forgetful functor, which forgets the action.

Each Hom⁡g(V,W)\Hom_\g(V,W)Homg​(V,W) is a subspace of Hom⁡C(V,W)\Hom_\C(V,W)HomC​(V,W), and composition is bilinear. The direct sum V⊕WV\oplus WV⊕W has action ρV(x)⊕ρW(x)\rho_V(x)\oplus\rho_W(x)ρV​(x)⊕ρW​(x). Its inclusions and projections are intertwiners, so it is a biproduct in C\mathcal CC. The zero module is a zero object.

Semisimplicity of g\gg now enters. By Weyl’s theorem (Lecture 5), every object of C\mathcal CC is a finite direct sum of simple objects. By Lecture 13, the simple objects are, up to isomorphism, the L(λ)L(\lambda)L(λ) with λ∈P+\lambda\in P_+λ∈P+​, and L(λ)≅L(μ)L(\lambda)\cong L(\mu)L(λ)≅L(μ) only if λ=μ\lambda=\muλ=μ.

The associativity, unit and flip maps are bijective, so their inverses also intertwine, by the argument used for Tˉ−1\bar T^{-1}Tˉ−1 in Proposition 14.1. Since FFF is faithful, a diagram of intertwiners commutes when its image under FFF does. Naturality and the pentagon, triangle and hexagon identities therefore hold because they hold in Vect⁡\operatorname{Vect}Vect. Hence C\mathcal CC is symmetric monoidal, and FFF is a symmetric monoidal functor with F(V⊗W)=F(V)⊗F(W)F(V\otimes W)=F(V)\otimes F(W)F(V⊗W)=F(V)⊗F(W).

These names record one concrete fact. Composites of associativity, unit and flip maps that induce the same permutation of labelled factors agree, after the stated unit and associativity identifications. For example, the pentagon says that both paths of associativity maps from ((u⊗v)⊗w)⊗x((u\otimes v)\otimes w)\otimes x((u⊗v)⊗w)⊗x to u⊗(v⊗(w⊗x))u\otimes(v\otimes(w\otimes x))u⊗(v⊗(w⊗x)) send this pure tensor to the displayed tensor. The hexagon says, with associativity maps suppressed, that moving uuu past vvv and then past www by two flips gives the same result as one flip of uuu past v⊗wv\otimes wv⊗w.

14.6 The structure of the category

Kernels, quotients, sums, tensor products and duals carry the module structures given above. The next terminology records their compatibility. In this terminology, abelian records kernels and cokernels, symmetric monoidal records the tensor product and the flip, and rigid records the duals with evaluation and coevaluation. Part II uses these constructions. It does not require memorising the names of the coherence diagrams.

A rigid symmetric tensor category is a C\CC-linear abelian symmetric monoidal category with finite-dimensional Hom spaces and objects of finite length, in which (S,T)↦S⊗T(S,T)\mapsto S\otimes T(S,T)↦S⊗T is bilinear, End⁡(1)=C\End(\mathbf 1)=\CEnd(1)=C, and every object has a left and a right dual.

Theorem 14.10 (Structure of C\mathcal CC). Let g\gg be complex semisimple. Then C\mathcal CC is a C\CC-linear semisimple abelian rigid symmetric tensor category with End⁡g(1)=C\End_\g(\mathbf 1)=\CEndg​(1)=C. The functor FFF is faithful, exact and tensor. If g≠0\g\ne0g=0, then C\mathcal CC has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects.

Proof. Propositions 14.1, 14.4 and 14.7 and the right duals give the stated structures. Weyl’s theorem gives semisimplicity. Hom spaces are finite-dimensional. Dimensions bound the length of strict chains of submodules. Bilinearity of (S,T)↦S⊗T(S,T)\mapsto S\otimes T(S,T)↦S⊗T holds for linear maps, hence for intertwiners. Every linear map C→C\C\to\CC→C intertwines, so End⁡g(1)=C\End_\g(\mathbf 1)=\CEndg​(1)=C. If g≠0\g\ne0g=0, then R≠∅R\ne\emptysetR=∅ and r≥1r\ge1r≥1. The weights mω1m\omega_1mω1​, m≥0m\ge0m≥0, are distinct and dominant, so the modules L(mω1)L(m\omega_1)L(mω1​) are pairwise nonisomorphic. 

If the zero algebra is admitted as semisimple, its category is Vect⁡\operatorname{Vect}Vect, with one simple object C\CC.

14.7 Part I in retrospect

Part I proved two classifications. Lectures 7–10 attach a connected Dynkin diagram to a complex simple Lie algebra.
