Lecture 7

Cartan subalgebras and root decomposition

We use simultaneous eigenspaces to study the structure of a semisimple Lie algebra. We take a maximal subalgebra of elements xxx with ad⁡x\ad xadx diagonalisable. Its adjoint eigenspaces for nonzero weights are the root spaces. The Killing form pairs opposite root spaces.

Throughout, g\gg is a nonzero semisimple complex Lie algebra with Killing form κ\kappaκ. By Lecture 4, κ\kappaκ is symmetric, nondegenerate and invariant: κ([x,y],z)=κ(x,[y,z])for all x,y,z∈g.\kappa([x,y],z)=\kappa(x,[y,z])\quad\text{for all }x,y,z\in\g.κ([x,y],z)=κ(x,[y,z])for all x,y,z∈g. The centre Z(g)Z(\g)Z(g) is an abelian ideal. Abelian ideals are solvable, so Z(g)Z(\g)Z(g) lies in the radical, which is zero. Hence Z(g)=0.Z(\g)=0.Z(g)=0.

7.1 Toral subalgebras and imports

Definition 7.1 (Toral and Cartan subalgebras). A subalgebra t⊆g\mathfrak t\subseteq\gt⊆g is toral if every element of t\mathfrak tt is semisimple. A Cartan subalgebra of g\gg is a toral subalgebra not properly contained in another toral subalgebra.

The zero subspace is toral. Every toral subalgebra has dimension at most dim⁡g\dim\gdimg, so it lies in a maximal one. Hence Cartan subalgebras exist.

The next proof uses a fact from linear algebra. Let TTT be a diagonalisable operator with distinct eigenvalues c1,…,crc_1,\dots,c_rc1​,…,cr​, and let UUU be a TTT-invariant subspace. Then (T−c1)⋯(T−cr)=0⟹(T∣U−c1)⋯(T∣U−cr)=0.(T-c_1)\cdots(T-c_r)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (T|_U-c_1)\cdots(T|_U-c_r)=0.(T−c1​)⋯(T−cr​)=0⟹(T∣U​−c1​)⋯(T∣U​−cr​)=0. So the minimal polynomial of T∣UT|_UT∣U​ divides a product of distinct linear factors. Hence T∣UT|_UT∣U​ is diagonalisable.

7.5 Changing the Cartan subalgebra

Theorem 7.12 (Conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras[C]). Let g\gg be a semisimple complex Lie algebra, and let h\mathfrak hh and h′\mathfrak h'h′ be Cartan subalgebras. Then some automorphism φ\varphiφ of g\gg satisfies φ(h)=h′\varphi(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h'φ(h)=h′.

Source: Humphreys §16.4, Corollary (conjugacy under the group E(L)E(L)E(L)). Humphreys defines Cartan subalgebras as nilpotent self-normalising subalgebras. By Humphreys §15.3, Corollary, these are exactly the maximal toral subalgebras when g\gg is semisimple.

The theorem and the transport argument show that the root decompositions of any two Cartan subalgebras are related by an automorphism of g\gg. Their common dimension is the rank of g\gg. Thus the classification in Lecture 10 does not depend on the choice.

Summary

A Cartan subalgebra is a maximal toral subalgebra. It is abelian, and by the cited self-centraliser theorem it equals its centraliser. Its adjoint eigenspaces give g=h⊕⨁α∈Rgα,[gα,gβ]⊆gα+β.\g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\g_\alpha, \qquad [\g_\alpha,\g_\beta]\subseteq\g_{\alpha+\beta}.g=h⊕α∈R⨁​gα​,[gα​,gβ​]⊆gα+β​.

The Killing form pairs gα\g_\alphagα​ only with g−α\g_{-\alpha}g−α​, and it is nondegenerate on h\mathfrak hh. The roots span h∗\mathfrak h^*h∗, and [gα,g−α]=Ctα[\g_\alpha,\g_{-\alpha}]=\C t_\alpha[gα​,g−α​]=Ctα​. Conjugacy makes the Cartan dimension independent of the choice. Lecture 8 builds on this.
