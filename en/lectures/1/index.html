Lie algebras, ideals and representations

What this lecture does

Matrix commutators lead to the definition of a Lie algebra. We study ideals, quotients and representations through three small examples. Unlike complex representations of finite groups, modules of a Lie algebra need not split into irreducible submodules.

Throughout this lecture, vector spaces and Lie algebras are finite-dimensional over C\CC. Products of linear maps mean composition. The product ABABAB applies BBB first.

1.1 The bracket

For two endomorphisms AAA and BBB of a vector space VVV, define their commutator by [A,B]=AB−BA.[A,B]=AB-BA.[A,B]=AB−BA. The commutator measures the failure of AAA and BBB to commute. It is bilinear and satisfies [A,B]=−[B,A][A,B]=-[B,A][A,B]=−[B,A].

Associativity of composition gives another identity. Expand the three double commutators: [A,[B,C]]=ABC−ACB−BCA+CBA,[B,[C,A]]=BCA−BAC−CAB+ACB,[C,[A,B]]=CAB−CBA−ABC+BAC.\begin{aligned} [A,[B,C]]&=ABC-ACB-BCA+CBA,\\ [B,[C,A]]&=BCA-BAC-CAB+ACB,\\ [C,[A,B]]&=CAB-CBA-ABC+BAC. \end{aligned}[A,[B,C]][B,[C,A]][C,[A,B]]​=ABC−ACB−BCA+CBA,=BCA−BAC−CAB+ACB,=CAB−CBA−ABC+BAC.​ Each of the six products occurs once with each sign. Hence [A,[B,C]]+[B,[C,A]]+[C,[A,B]]=0.[A,[B,C]]+[B,[C,A]]+[C,[A,B]]=0.[A,[B,C]]+[B,[C,A]]+[C,[A,B]]=0. This is the Jacobi identity.

Definition 1.1 (Lie algebra). A Lie algebra is a complex vector space g\gg with a bilinear map [ ⋅ , ⋅ ] ⁣:g×g⟶g[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]\colon\g\times\g\longrightarrow\g[⋅,⋅]:g×g⟶g such that [x,y]=−[y,x],[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0for all x,y,z∈g.[x,y]=-[y,x], \qquad [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0 \quad\text{for all }x,y,z\in\g.[x,y]=−[y,x],[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0for all x,y,z∈g. The map is the Lie bracket.

Over C\CC, antisymmetry gives 2[x,x]=02[x,x]=02[x,x]=0, so [x,x]=0[x,x]=0[x,x]=0. Conversely, bilinearity and [x,x]=0[x,x]=0[x,x]=0 imply antisymmetry by expanding [x+y,x+y][x+y,x+y][x+y,x+y].

The vector space End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) with the commutator bracket is denoted gl(V)\gl(V)gl(V). A basis of VVV identifies gl(V)\gl(V)gl(V) with all complex n×nn\times nn×n matrices, where n=dim⁡Vn=\dim Vn=dimV. We denote this Lie algebra by gln(C)\gl_n(\C)gln​(C).

Definition 1.2 (Subalgebra and abelian algebra). A vector subspace a⊆g\alga\subseteq\ga⊆g is a Lie subalgebra if [x,y]∈afor all x,y∈a.[x,y]\in\alga\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\alga.[x,y]∈afor all x,y∈a. A Lie algebra is abelian if every bracket is zero.

Every vector space becomes an abelian Lie algebra by giving it the zero bracket.

1.2 Ideals and quotients

For subspaces U,W⊆gU,W\subseteq\gU,W⊆g, write [U,W]=span⁡{[u,w]:u∈U, w∈W}.[U,W]=\Span\{[u,w]:u\in U,\ w\in W\}.[U,W]=span{[u,w]:u∈U, w∈W}. This notation means the span of the brackets, not just the set of brackets.

Definition 1.4 (Ideal, centre and derived algebra). An ideal of g\gg is a vector subspace a\algaa such that [g,a]⊆a.[\g,\alga]\subseteq\alga.[g,a]⊆a. 1.3 The adjoint representation

A Lie algebra acts on its own vector space through the bracket. For x∈gx\in\gx∈g, define ad⁡x ⁣:g⟶g,(ad⁡x)(y)=[x,y].\ad x\colon\g\longrightarrow\g, \qquad (\ad x)(y)=[x,y].adx:g⟶g,(adx)(y)=[x,y]. Hence ad⁡ ⁣:g⟶gl(g)\ad\colon\g\longrightarrow\gl(\g)ad:g⟶gl(g) is a Lie algebra homomorphism. It is the adjoint representation. Definition 1.7 (Derivation). A derivation of g\gg is a linear map D ⁣:g→gD\colon\g\to\gD:g→g such that D([y,z])=[Dy,z]+[y,Dz]for all y,z∈g.D([y,z])=[Dy,z]+[y,Dz] \quad\text{for all }y,z\in\g.D([y,z])=[Dy,z]+[y,Dz]for all y,z∈g. A derivation of the form ad⁡x\ad xadx is an inner derivation.

Equation (1.1) says that every ad⁡x\ad xadx is a derivation. This is the bracket version of the product rule.

In a nonzero abelian algebra, both sides of the derivation rule vanish. Thus every linear map is a derivation, while every inner derivation is zero.

1.4 Representations and invariant subspaces

Definition 1.9 (Representation). A representation of g\gg on VVV is a Lie algebra homomorphism ρ ⁣:g⟶gl(V).\rho\colon\g\longrightarrow\gl(V).ρ:g⟶gl(V). A group representation turns products into products of operators. A Lie algebra representation turns brackets into commutators of operators. The operators ρ(x)\rho(x)ρ(x) of a Lie algebra representation need not be invertible.

Summary

A Lie bracket is bilinear and antisymmetric, and it satisfies the Jacobi identity. Ideals are exactly the subspaces for which the bracket of cosets is well defined. The centre is the kernel of the adjoint representation.

Representations turn brackets into commutators. Intertwiners between finite-dimensional irreducible complex modules satisfy Schur’s lemma. A module need not be completely reducible. The next two lectures define nilpotency and solvability. They apply these definitions to the triangular examples.
